\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Sparse PCA and multi-component estimation}
Regression formulations, semidefinite relaxations, penalized matrix decompositions, Fantope methods, and truncated power algorithms provide distinct trade-offs among statistical guarantees, exact sparsity, computation, and component coupling \citep{zou2006sparse,daspremont2007direct,daspremont2008optimal,witten2009penalized,vu2013fantope,yuan2013truncated}. Recent work has improved polynomial-time sample complexity in spiked models and strengthened exact or certifiable optimization \citep{cai2025fast,bertsimas2022certifiable}. Our contribution is not a new sparsity penalty. It is a correction of the block value function, a precise separation of two estimators, and an analysis of the discrete-to-smooth dynamics induced by thresholding and Stiefel projection.

Several modern multi-component methods directly optimize orthogonal or nonredundant sparse components. Those approaches raise the empirical standard for any block method: a corrected formulation must be compared with joint estimators, not only with deflation. We therefore distinguish score orthogonality, loading orthogonality, score decorrelation, support overlap, and causal nonredundancy. Only score-side Stiefel orthogonality is built into the primary model; the others are measured or added as optional penalties.

\subsection{Convex maximization and generalized Frank--Wolfe}
The historical update maximizes an affine minorant of a convex objective over a compact nonconvex set. This is related to unit-step conditional-gradient or generalized Frank--Wolfe schemes for convex maximization. \citet{aktas2025strongly} establish last-iterate results under smoothness, strong convexity, and a KŁ condition. That analysis does not automatically settle the present block problem: the corrected objective need not satisfy the same strong-convexity hypotheses, the Stiefel feasible geometry differs from a strongly convex set, and zero-response or rank-deficient polar steps require explicit policies. We use KŁ machinery only after establishing sufficient ascent and a relative-error bound for the actual safeguarded algorithm.

\subsection{Sparse optimization on matrix manifolds}
Manifold proximal-gradient, retraction, and alternating methods address nonsmooth composite problems under orthogonality constraints \citep{absil2008optimization}. FACET differs by eliminating a high-dimensional sparse block to produce a thresholded value function, preserving the historical full-polar update for comparison, and analyzing component-specific active operators after identification. The closest future-facing comparison is sparse-Stiefel work that combines identification with Newton acceleration. Such methods may dominate the local phase when second-order sufficient conditions and clean active-manifold intersections hold. We therefore treat second-order acceleration as a stretch result and do not claim it generically for heterogeneous active supports.

\subsection{Sparse neural representations}
Sparse autoencoders learn overcomplete dictionaries rather than orthogonal spectral components. Gated SAEs separate support selection from magnitude estimation and improve the sparsity--reconstruction frontier \citep{rajamanoharan2024gated}. MIB and SAEBench show why reconstruction and feature labels are insufficient: causal localization, stability, disentanglement, and downstream interventions must be evaluated directly \citep{mueller2025mib,karvonen2025saebench}. Recent analysis also identifies an amortization gap between simple encoders and more accurate sparse inference \citep{oneill2025compute}. The activation experiment reported in \Cref{sec:results} compares methods at matched support size and explicitly distinguishes raw selection from refitting.
