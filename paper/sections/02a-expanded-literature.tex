\section{Expanded Literature Synthesis and Positioning}
\label{sec:expanded-related}
The relevant literature separates into four lines that should not be conflated. First, sparse principal-component methods differ in whether sparsity is imposed on loadings, covariance surrogates, subspace projectors, or low-rank factors. The formulation studied here belongs to the loading-selection family, but its score-space reduction and polar block update distinguish it from regression surrogates and convex projector relaxations \citep{zou2006sparse,daspremont2007direct,vu2013fantope}. Second, truncated and thresholded power methods provide exact-cardinality or thresholded iterations, but they do not by themselves resolve the aggregation mismatch, inactive-column issue, or refitting inconsistency in the historical block construction \citep{yuan2013truncated}.

Third, nonsmooth optimization on manifolds supplies general convergence tools, including KŁ-based finite-length arguments, but a generic theorem is not a substitute for verifying the exact objective, the implemented retraction, sufficient ascent, relative error, and rank-deficiency policy \citep{absil2008optimization,attouch2013convergence,bolte2007lojasiewicz,aktas2025strongly}. Fourth, modern neural representation analysis provides a demanding application domain in which supports, component redundancy, and causal relevance can be evaluated, but reconstruction quality alone is not evidence of interpretable or causally useful structure \citep{mueller2025mib,rajamanoharan2024gated,karvonen2025saebench}.

The paper's novelty claim is consequently narrow and testable: it reconstructs two mathematically coherent block estimators, specifies algorithms whose edge cases are explicit, establishes exact algebraic and conditional identification results, and develops the proof obligations needed for whole-sequence and post-identification theory. The neural application is a stress test of those mechanisms, not the source of their validity.
