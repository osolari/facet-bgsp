\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The block generalized-power construction becomes mathematically coherent only after separating two objectives, permitting inactive columns, and correcting the refit. That reconstruction exposes a two-phase algorithm: discrete threshold discovery followed by smooth conditional spectral dynamics. The paper's strongest contribution is the combination of exact model repair, explicit numerical policies, formal whole-sequence convergence of the safeguarded method via the Kurdyka--\L{}ojasiewicz property on a semi-algebraic objective, finite identification under strict margins, correctly scoped block local analysis, and safe late-stage screening with a measured solver-level realization. The modern-AI experiments on GPT-2 small residual streams, vision-transformer CLS tokens, and the classical Pitprops sparse-PCA benchmark show that the same thresholded block-sparse estimator carries interpretability and reconstruction benefits across modalities without the reconstruction cost of a sparse-autoencoder dictionary of matching width. The architecture of the result (proofs anchored to a specific solver, numerical conventions anchored to specific tests, and experiments anchored to deterministic configuration files) is designed to make the paper's claims directly auditable alongside the reference implementation.
