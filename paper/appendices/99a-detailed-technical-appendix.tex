\section{Detailed Proofs, Counterexamples, and Numerical Contracts}
\label{app:a-detailed}

\subsection{Counterexample: normalize then mask}
Let $v=(1,1)^\top/\sqrt2$ and retain only the first coordinate. Normalizing first and masking gives $(1/\sqrt2,0)^\top$, whose norm is $1/\sqrt2$, not one. Masking first and normalizing gives $(1,0)^\top$. Thus any unit-sphere restricted loading update must mask before normalization.

\subsection{Counterexample: threshold ties}
Let $a^\top x_\star=\gamma$ and choose $x_k\to x_\star$ with $a^\top x_k-\gamma=(-1)^k/k$. The support alternates forever while the iterates converge. Strict threshold separation is therefore necessary for finite identification by continuity alone.

\subsection{Polar differential}
\label{app:a-polar}
Let $G=UP$ be the polar decomposition with $P\succ0$. For a perturbation $H$, write $D\operatorname{Polar}(G)[H]=U\Omega+(I-UU^\top)HP^{-1}$, where $\Omega$ is skew-symmetric and solves
\[
P\Omega+\Omega P=U^\top H-H^\top U.
\]
The Sylvester equation is uniquely solvable because the eigenvalues of $P$ are positive. Composing this differential with $DG(X)$ gives the exact linearization of the heterogeneous-support map used in the local-rate diagnostic.

\subsection{Proof contract for the KŁ theorem}
A complete proof must verify: compactness of the iterate set; smoothness and Lipschitz constants for the selected retraction chart; sufficient ascent with an accepted step bounded away from zero when the gradient is bounded away from zero; a relative-error inequality in the Riemannian normal cone; definability of the extended objective; and compatibility of the rank-deficiency rule with these constants. Failure of any item downgrades the result to subsequential stationarity.
