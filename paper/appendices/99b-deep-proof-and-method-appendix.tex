\section{Whole-Sequence Proof under Explicit Algorithmic Assumptions}
\label{app:a-kl-proof}
This appendix records a proof-complete abstract convergence result and isolates the paper-specific verification tasks. Let $\mathcal M=\St(p,m)$ with a smooth retraction $R$. Let $\Psi=-F+\iota_{\mathcal M}$, and suppose an algorithm generates $X_{k+1}=R_{X_k}(\eta_k)$.

\begin{assumption}[Abstract safeguarded-step conditions]
\label{ass:a-abstract-kl}
There are constants $a,b>0$ such that for all sufficiently large $k$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item $\Psi(X_{k+1})+a\norm{\eta_k}_F^2\le\Psi(X_k)$;
\item there exists $W_{k+1}\in\partial\Psi(X_{k+1})$ with $\norm{W_{k+1}}_F\le b\norm{\eta_k}_F$;
\item $\norm{X_{k+1}-X_k}_F\le c_R\norm{\eta_k}_F$ and the reverse bound holds locally;
\item the iterates lie in a compact set and $\Psi$ is a KŁ function there.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Finite length and last-iterate convergence]
\label{thm:a-abstract-kl}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:a-abstract-kl}, if the sequence has an accumulation point $X_\star$, then
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\norm{X_{k+1}-X_k}_F<\infty,
\]
and $X_k\to X_\star$, where $0\in\partial\Psi(X_\star)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sufficient decrease of $\Psi$ implies convergence of objective values and square summability of $\eta_k$. Let $\Omega$ be the compact limit set. Standard continuity arguments show that $\Psi$ is constant on $\Omega$ and every limit point is critical once the relative-error sequence tends to zero. Uniformization of the KŁ inequality provides a concave desingularizing function $\varphi$ and a neighborhood of $\Omega$ such that
\[
\varphi'(\Psi(X)-\Psi_\star)\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial\Psi(X))\ge1.
\]
Concavity of $\varphi$, sufficient decrease, and relative error yield
\[
\varphi(\Psi(X_k)-\Psi_\star)-\varphi(\Psi(X_{k+1})-\Psi_\star)
\ge c\norm{\eta_k}_F
\]
for a positive constant $c$ after the sequence enters the neighborhood. Telescoping gives $\sum_k\norm{\eta_k}_F<\infty$, hence finite iterate length by retraction equivalence. The sequence is Cauchy and converges. Closedness of the limiting subdifferential and $W_k\to0$ imply criticality.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Paper-specific verification.}
The theorem becomes a result about the implemented safeguarded FACET method only after proving: a uniform retraction descent lemma for $-F_{\mathrm{sq}}$; Armijo termination; a lower bound on accepted steps or a step-dependent relative-error inequality; compatibility of the regularized rank-deficient update with the same constants; and definability of the extended objective. These are not delegated to the abstract theorem.

\section{Derivation of the Polar Differential}
\label{app:a-polar-proof}
Let $G=UP$ with $U^\top U=I$ and $P\succ0$. Differentiate $G=UP$ in direction $H$:
\[
H=\dot U P+U\dot P.
\]
Left multiplication by $U^\top$ gives $U^\top H=U^\top\dot U P+\dot P$. The matrix $\Omega=U^\top\dot U$ is skew-symmetric because differentiating $U^\top U=I$ yields $\Omega+\Omega^\top=0$. Since $\dot P$ is symmetric, subtracting the transpose gives the Sylvester equation
\[
P\Omega+\Omega P=U^\top H-H^\top U.
\]
Projecting the differentiated factorization onto the orthogonal complement of $U$ gives
\[
(I-UU^\top)H=(I-UU^\top)\dot U P,
\]
so
\[
\dot U=U\Omega+(I-UU^\top)HP^{-1}.
\]
Because $P\succ0$, the Sylvester operator $\Omega\mapsto P\Omega+\Omega P$ is invertible on skew-symmetric matrices. This formula is used for the heterogeneous-support Jacobian and for numerical derivative checks.

\section{Support-Specific Hessian Calculations}
On a fixed support-and-sign cell, $F_{\mathrm{sq}}$ is quadratic plus linear terms. For component $j$ with active support $S_j$ and sign vector $s_j$,
\[
f_j(x)=\mu_j^2x^\top A_{S_j}A_{S_j}^\top x
-2\mu_j\gamma_j s_j^\top A_{S_j}^\top x
+\gamma_j^2\abs{S_j}.
\]
The ambient Hessian is $2\mu_j^2A_{S_j}A_{S_j}^\top$. The Riemannian Hessian additionally includes curvature terms from the orthogonality constraint. At a critical point, tangent quadratic forms determine whether the point is a local maximizer, saddle, or degenerate. The paper computes these forms explicitly in the rank-one and common-operator block cases and uses numerical eigenanalysis for heterogeneous supports.

\section{Extended Counterexample Catalogue}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Aggregation mismatch:} construct two Stiefel frames whose response vectors reverse ordering under $\ell_1$ versus squared aggregation.
\item \textbf{Inactive sphere contradiction:} demonstrate that the zero loading is infeasible under a unit-sphere column constraint despite being selected by the positive-part value function.
\item \textbf{Masking error:} quantify norm and objective loss from normalize-then-mask.
\item \textbf{Threshold cycling:} exhibit convergence to a threshold boundary with infinite support switching.
\item \textbf{Rank-deficient polar input:} show nonunique score updates and sensitivity to branch selection.
\item \textbf{Heterogeneous supports:} show that a pooled eigengap can predict contraction while the true coupled polar Jacobian is unstable.
\end{enumerate}
Each counterexample has a deterministic unit test and a manuscript-level explanation of the assumption it invalidates.
